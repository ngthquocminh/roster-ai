- (capability) CAP-1 intent: An authenticated seeded planner can enter one site-scoped workspace and select an immutable predefined fixture. Success: unauthenticated or cross-site access and any second user or active membership are rejected, while the chosen fixture and versions remain explicit.
- (capability) CAP-2 intent: The planner can inspect the normalized scenario facts and stable identifiers available to agent investigation. Success: the read-only viewer and agent inspection projection match for every Gate A fixture and expose no supported mutation path.
- (capability) CAP-3 intent: The planner can create, revisit, and use durable conversations to investigate schedule problems with grounded agent assistance. Success: reconnect reconstructs ordered state, ambiguous or unauthorized requests clarify or refuse, and every numerical or schedule claim resolves to saved version-bound evidence.
- (capability) CAP-4 intent: The planner can turn operational intent into a validated, reversible schedule-change draft. Success: resolved entities, constraints or objectives, locks, expected versions, and consequences are reviewable or rejectable without changing the baseline, and invalid inputs fail before computation.
- (capability) CAP-5 intent: The planner can obtain deterministic schedule candidates and feasibility decisions from versioned proposal inputs. Success: CP-SAT alone constructs or validates accepted assignments, every completed feasible candidate satisfies hard constraints and locks, and infeasible work creates no promotable candidate.
- (capability) CAP-6 intent: The planner can explicitly start, monitor, cancel, leave, and resume bounded optimization work. Success: accepted work has one durable run ID, survives browser or worker interruption, exposes distinct terminal states, enforces positive application-owned budgets, and retries create no duplicate effect.
- (capability) CAP-7 intent: The planner can compare a candidate with its exact baseline before deciding. Success: the comparison shows worker, shift, task, interval coverage, overtime, cost or objective, constraint, version, and unresolved-gap deltas without substituting missing evidence.
- (capability) CAP-8 intent: The planner can explicitly approve an exact feasible candidate as the ShiftMind operational baseline. Success: stale, expired, replayed, altered, or mismatched approval fails closed, while a valid decision atomically consumes approval, changes the baseline pointer once, writes audit evidence, and preserves prior versions.
- (capability) CAP-9 intent: The planner and reviewer can reconstruct the complete governed decision path. Success: provenance links request, evidence, concise decision summary, tool and policy outcomes, solver run, approval, execution, and before or after versions without hidden chain-of-thought; consequential outcomes are unsampled and append-only.
- (capability) CAP-10 intent: Product engineers can add governed agent capabilities without widening core orchestration authority. Success: a versioned module can be registered and removed without editing the agent loop, remains unavailable by default, and cannot execute without typed contracts, scope, risk, budgets, audit mapping, policy, and evaluations.
- (capability) CAP-11 intent: The planner can keep using inspectable saved work and the manual deterministic solver path when the model or optional telemetry is unavailable. Success: model or Logfire failure causes no state or audit corruption and disables only the dependent assistance or export surface.
- (capability) CAP-12 intent: The team can release and operate the portfolio MVP reproducibly with executable evidence. Success: deterministic, browser, security, recovery, accessibility, backup or restore, and rollback gates pass against version-bound reports before the AWS portfolio release.
- (constraint) The model interprets and orchestrates only; application controls own identity, site scope, authorization, policy, versions, budgets, approval, idempotency, persistence, and audit, while CP-SAT owns accepted schedule construction and feasibility.
- (constraint) The MVP permits one pre-provisioned planner and one active site membership, disables public registration, and derives authority from the authenticated server session rather than client or model values.
- (constraint) Scenario source data is limited to immutable predefined fixtures; no upload, create, import, edit, or delete path is allowed in the MVP.
- (constraint) Analysis, draft, explicit run, comparison, exact approval, and baseline promotion remain distinct states and controls; stale state is never silently rebased.
- (constraint) All accepted work, events, versions, evidence, and effects are durable and idempotent; browser streams, process memory, model SDKs, and telemetry are not recovery boundaries.
- (constraint) Every numerical or schedule-specific claim and comparison is recomputable from immutable version-bound evidence; unsupported numbers fail the grounding gate.
- (constraint) Authoritative business audit and product state remain unsampled under ShiftMind control and separate from sanitized operational logs and optional external traces.
- (constraint) Sensitive workforce, prompt, schedule, approval, and credential content is excluded from external telemetry by default; secrets never enter prompts, browser payloads, audit summaries, logs, traces, or evaluation fixtures.
- (constraint) The exact architecture, stack, contract shapes, state machines, persistence roles, brownfield cutover, AWS boundaries, and migration rules are governed by adopted ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md.
- (constraint) The exact routes, interaction states, evidence navigation, responsive behavior, keyboard behavior, and accessibility floor are governed by adopted EXPERIENCE.md.
- (constraint) The visual identity and component deltas are governed by adopted DESIGN.md and must not imply authority, confidence, editability, or merged action levels.
- (constraint) Gate A must deliver the tenant and fixture spine plus read-only viewer and parity tests before AgentRuntime or tool orchestration; Gate B then adds production-shaped durability and AWS proof without weakening invariants.
- (constraint) Normal release evaluation is deterministic-first; authorization, approval, isolation, hard-constraint, grounding, idempotency, authoritative-audit, viewer parity, recovery, accessibility, backup or restore, and rollback regressions block release.
- (constraint) Customer-grade latency, availability, concurrency, recovery, retention, residency, compliance, and cost claims remain unset until measured traffic or a target customer makes them concrete.
- (decision) Non-goals include SaaS administration, multiple active users or separation of duties, billing, broad WFM features, forecasting or WMS execution, network-wide optimization, autonomous promotion, unrestricted tools, generalized RAG, multi-agent orchestration, custom scenario mutation, and exhaustive labor-rule fidelity.
