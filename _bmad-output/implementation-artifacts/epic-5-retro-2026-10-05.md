### What went well

1. **Decision-grade review findings moved to story creation, and stayed there.** 5.5 had 2 and 5.7
   had 3. From 5.8 to 5.12, five consecutive stories had **zero**. Two practices did it: the
   "N decisions were made at story creation — do not re-litigate them" section, and measuring at
   creation. For example, 5.9 measured real spans to settle the export allow-list, and 5.10 measured
   the Logfire SDK's own leak channels before choosing an export path. This is the review cost that
   matters most in this project.
2. **Live evaluation went from never having run to being a gated regression baseline.** It went
   3/30 (09-10) → 26/26 single-turn → 6/6 multi-turn → 87/90 conversations → 129/129. It now has a
   committed baseline, a drop check, Logfire publication, and per-turn expectations graded on the
   action rather than the wording.
3. **Cheap first, then pay.** 5.12's recorded run cost USD 0.08. Offline Jev replay of recorded
   replies, deliberately broken replies, and single-scenario smokes found the failures before the
   full run.

### Challenges

1. **5.7 was the expensive story.** It had 55 patch findings and a correct-course in the middle of
   the story (8 scenarios / 819 turns right-sized to 3 / 30). The agent model changed three times
   (DeepSeek Flash → Haiku 4.5 → gpt-5.6-luna), and the spend ceiling was raised without being
   recorded, which became one of its decision findings. Root cause: the acceptance criteria were
   written before anyone measured what a live suite costs to run, or whether the configured model
   could do the work.
2. **Epic 4 A1 failed its done-when again.** See §2.
3. **Gate B has still not been assessed.** See §4. The reader-facing docs (`README.md:161`,
   `docs/WALKTHROUGH.md:88`) still say "30 cases"; the dataset has 37.
4. **The ledger is filling with untriggered deferrals.** Since Story 5.5, `deferred-work.md` has
   gained 47 entries marked "Owner: open", and only 21 revisit-trigger lines. An entry with no
   trigger is never picked up by the retrospective's routed-work scan, so in practice it has been
   dropped.

### Epic 4 A1: closed, with the reviewer re-run as the mechanism

The strengthened dev-side mechanism committed on 09-10 never shipped; there has been no commit to
`_bmad/custom/` since. Six of the eight stories raised a "mutation table missing rows" or "guard
cannot go red" finding at review: 5.5, 5.6, 5.7, 5.8, 5.9 and 5.11. They come in two kinds:

- **Paperwork:** rows missing for guards that work. Examples: 5.5's table format, 5.8's three
  untabled AC-level tests, 5.11's roughly 25 untabled guards.
- **Real weak guards:**
  - 5.7's `empty_answer` guard: replacing it with `if False:` left 54 tests green.
  - 5.7's override-digest test: it stayed green with the hash replaced by a constant.
  - 5.9's four canary cells: they assert on surfaces that are never populated.
  - 5.6: two guards with surviving mutants.

**Every one was patch-grade, and the reviewer's independent re-run (the paired
`bmad-code-review.toml` fact) caught every one; none got past review.** The dev side is not
useless either: 5.11's own table found a weak guard before review, and 5.10 and 5.12 were clean.

Decision (Minh): **close A1.** The two-step system (dev table, then reviewer re-run) is the
mechanism. Its goal is that no weak guard gets past review, and that goal is being met. The "zero
findings at review" done-when was a secondary target, and three epics of prose rules have not moved
it. Both custom facts stay unchanged. Do not reopen A1 for missing-row findings caught at review;
reopen it only if a weak guard gets past review into merged code.
