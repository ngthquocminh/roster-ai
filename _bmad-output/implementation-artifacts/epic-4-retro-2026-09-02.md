### What went well

1. **Four of six Epic 3 commitments landed clean, and one of them caught a real error.** The
   production-supplier rule (ADR-4 D8 / spine EAD-9) did not merely get followed — it forced a
   correction at review, when Story 4.1's completion note claimed `schedule_assignment` rows as a
   supplier and the actual read was `schedule_version.payload`. A rule that changes an outcome is
   worth more than a rule that gets ticked.
2. **The design tier arrived before the implementation tier, for the first time.** ADR-4, the
   Epic 4 architecture spine and the seven verification obligations existed before Story 4.1 was
   created, exactly as the Epic 3 retrospective's critical path required. ADR-4 carries an explicit
   "Open questions (not silently answered)" section.
3. **A declared gap fired its own revisit trigger on time.** Story 4.4 recorded
   `SCOPE_CONTROLS[4] = "audit_evidence_refs:empty_at_every_write_site"` with the ledger entry
   naming the trigger — *"decide before Story 4.5 is created, because 4.5 AC3 must not prove
   evidence integrity vacuously against a structurally empty audit field."* The trigger fired,
   produced `sprint-change-proposal-2026-09-01.md`, and inserted Story 4.4a. This is the
   deferred-work ledger working as designed rather than accumulating.
4. **The mid-epic authoring fix held.** See §3.
5. **Review found blast radius the story could not see.** Story 4.3's review caught that the first
   successful promotion would permanently 409 the result endpoint site-wide — *"Epic 4's own loop —
   approve, promote, approve again — is a one-way door after one use"* — a defect invisible from
   the diff and only reachable by reasoning about the second execution.

### Challenges and lessons

- **Decision-grade cost concentrates in first-of-a-mechanism stories and in proof stories.** 4.1
  (first approval path) = 2, 4.2 (first decision endpoint) = 4, 4.6 (proof of everything) = 5.
  Stories that extend an established mechanism (4.4, 4.4a) = 0. The middle of an epic is cheap; its
  ends are not.
- **The recurring failure mode did not change, it relocated.** Epic 3 named it: *"a guard that
  passes without proving a meaningful failure."* Epic 4's early decision-grade cost was authoring
  drift; its late decision-grade cost is proof vacuity. Fixing the story-authoring net moved the
  cost downstream rather than removing it.
- **Planning records still drift against code.** Story 4.2's review found `deferred-work.md:526`
  marked CLOSED on a proof production cannot reproduce. `:512` and `:524` remain open and untouched.
  `epic-4` is still `in-progress` in sprint-status with all seven stories `done`.
- **The release-blocking artifact can go stale without anything failing.** Story 4.6's review found `approval_and_audit_invariants` absent from the committed Gate A readiness report for a
  whole story, and no test compares the committed report against the live registry. A gate
  that reports on a stale copy of itself is the same category of defect as a guard that
  cannot go red — one layer up.
- **An AC can name a subject that does not exist.** Story 4.5's AC3 required proof against "the
  local create-only evidence adapter"; no evidence or object-storage port exists in the codebase.
  The premise had already been softened once (`sprint-change-proposal-2026-08-09-epics-2-5.md`)
  without checking that a subject for it existed — a second-order failure, corrected properly this
  time by removing the premise rather than softening it a third time.

### Key insight

> Epic 4's story-authoring net worked — the rules shipped mid-epic measurably held for four
> consecutive stories. But fixing where a story contradicts *itself* moved the decision-grade cost
> downstream to where a **guard does not test the product**, and that action item has been "in
> progress" since Epic 1–2. It keeps failing not because the rule is ignored, but because the
> rule's wording accepts a red that proves nothing.

## 3. The mechanism that held

Story 4.2 shipped three authoring rules into `_bmad/custom/bmad-create-story.toml` (commit
`2cf598f`, 2026-08-30) and handed this retrospective an explicit, bounded job:

> *"The retro's job is to judge whether they held across 4.3–4.6, not to re-derive the diagnosis."*

The three rules: a Task cites a Decision rather than re-arguing it; a Decision states what its
mechanism does NOT cover; and a self-consistency pass before freezing, distinct from the existing
facts pass.

**Verdict: they held.** Story 4.2 produced three restatement-drift findings out of four
decision-grade findings. Stories 4.3, 4.4, 4.4a, 4.5 and 4.6 produced **zero**. Story 4.3's change
log confirms it was created from "Story 4.2's inheritance section and review retrospective," so
the mechanism was consumed at the first opportunity. The action item's own stated condition — "no
restatement-drift finding in any of 4.3–4.6" — is met, and it moves to `done`.

**The declared blind spot recurred once, and stays open.** Story 4.2 recorded that the rules cannot
reach cross-document undefined-term propagation, because `epics.md`, the architecture spine and
ADR-4 all predate story creation. That shape appeared once in Epic 4, at Story 4.3: *"EAD-5 and
Decision 8 do not settle whether a resumed turn may suspend again."* One occurrence, caught at
review, resolved by refusing chained approvals explicitly. The half is unowned and this
retrospective assigns it (action A2).

## 4. The mechanism that did not, and the specific reason

All five of Story 4.6's decision-grade findings are one claim in five forms — **the proof surface
does not test the product**:

- *"Pairwise distinctness cannot go red — the harness supplies the discriminator, not the product."*
  `roleNameTree` read the fixture wrapper's own `aria-label`, so distinctness was true by
  construction for all 60 entries.
- *"33 of 60 matrix states render hand-written prose; no feature component is ever mounted."*
  Reproduced by the reviewer: three UX-DR32-prohibited classes placed on `DraftCard`'s shipped
  root — the matrix stayed green.
- The remaining three concern a disabled-button treatment that exceeded what Decision 10
  authorised, both resolved at review.

The same family, one story earlier, in Story 4.5: *"AC3's absence clause is asserted nowhere; the
only test near it asserts the opposite branch."* And in Story 4.1: *"Decision 8's both-producer
agreement — the whole point of Task 3 — has no seeded-row test."*

### Why the rule keeps failing

It is not a discipline failure. **Story 4.6's Task 4 states the rule verbatim and correctly.** The
dev then satisfied it with a red from *incomplete code* — an unresolved import, a first draft.

That is the natural red of test-first development, and the rule as currently written admits it. A
missing-import red proves the test file loads. A mutation red proves the guard has teeth. Those are
different claims, and only the second is what "demonstrated red" was ever intended to mean.

### Why this one is fixable where the restatement rules were not

The Epic 1–2 and Epic 4 authoring rules were deliberately not backed by tests, because every
available assertion is a proxy. **This rule is different: it leaves an artifact.** Story 4.6's
reviewer produced exactly the artifact in question — a mutation table with columns *mutation
applied to real code | guard that should redden | before | after*.

If the Dev Agent Record must carry that table before a story reaches review, a vacuous guard is
visible without a reviewer having to re-derive it. That is action A1.

## 5. Epic 5 preparation

Epic 5 — *Demonstrable Local Planner Workspace* — is the portfolio milestone: agent-run
instrumentation for latency/budget/cost (5.1), content and secret leak prevention (5.2), a
one-command reproducible run (5.3), and the published walkthrough (5.4).

**Nothing in Epic 4 blocks Epic 5 starting.** Story 5.1's approval-age telemetry has its producer;
5.4's Gate A evidence artifacts exist.

**Epic 5 does not inherit the subject-vacuity defect.** This was checked directly, because Story
4.5's AC3 and AC4 both named subjects that do not exist and the same failure reaching Epic 5's
telemetry stories would be expensive. Stories 5.1–5.4 name no absent subject: 5.1 asks for
"structured JSON logs and metrics", 5.2 for "any configured trace export", and
`backend/pyproject.toml:27-45` already records the Logfire SDK as "a stack row owned by Story 5.1"
with `opentelemetry-sdk` present but test-only. Every CloudWatch reference in `epics.md` sits in
Epic 3, Epic 4, or Epic 6, where it is a hosted concern.

**Four Epic 4 gaps do constrain Story 5.4**, whose acceptance criteria require that *"every claim
it makes about behavior is reproducible by the Story 5.3 command"* and that limitations be
explicit. Each needs a fix-or-disclose decision **before 5.4 is created**:

1. **The baseline half of every comparison is structurally empty.** Verified in code at this
   retrospective: `PostgresScenarioProjectionReader.get_baseline_assignments`
   (`backend/adapters/postgres/scenario_projection.py:637-650`) still applies its query to a
   literal `()`. Story 4.3's review found the consequence — the first successful promotion
   permanently 409'd the result endpoint site-wide — and resolved it by degrading the response
   gracefully, not by wiring a supply. The approve/promote/approve loop works; comparison *against
   a promoted baseline* does not. This fact has been in the record since 2026-08-16 (Epic 1–2
   action items) and is now load-bearing for the epic's headline capability.
2. **Story 4.6's state/accessibility proof is thinner than its name.** Its own honest gap (c):
   *"33 of the 60 entries render hand-written prose and mount no product component, so AC1's
   distinctness claim is not yet proven against shipped surfaces."* Two of the five decision
   findings were resolved by mounting the real components; the scope caveat remains. If 5.4 links
   this evidence, the caveat travels with it.
3. **No separation of duties on the decision endpoint.** Story 4.2's review, deferred: *"the
   requester can decide their own approval — deferred, pre-existing: no story owns separation of
   duties."* Defensible under one-planner MVP scope, but for a portfolio artifact whose thesis is a
   *governed* consequential workflow, it must be stated rather than discovered.
4. **The Gate A readiness report can go stale silently, and once did.** Story 4.6's review
   established that `approval_and_audit_invariants` — Story 4.5's own invariant — was **absent**
   from the committed `evidence/story-1.11/gate-a-readiness-report.json` at `8b2b5b1`, so the
   release-blocking artifact described a stale registry for a whole story before a later commit
   silently caught it up. Nothing in `backend/tests/test_gate_a_readiness.py` compares the
   committed report against the live `GATE_A_CHECKS` registry. Story 5.4 must link the Gate A
   evidence artifacts and claim reproducibility for every behavioural assertion; an artifact that
   can drift unnoticed cannot carry that claim. Compounding it, Story 4.6's Decision 11 fences
   every existing `evidence/story-*/` directory at a zero-line diff while its own Task 11 requires
   re-running Gate A, which rewrites exactly that file — a spec self-contradiction the
   implementation resolved without escalating.

## 8. Closing commitments

The team carries forward one sharpened commitment from three consecutive epics: **a guard counts
only when it has been made to fail by mutating finished code, and the mutation is recorded as an
artifact rather than asserted as a claim.** Epic 4 proved that a well-worded rule with an
unenforceable reading gets the unenforceable reading. Epic 5 tests whether naming the specific hole
closes it.

Epic 4 is complete from a story, quality, and stakeholder-acceptance perspective. Epic 5 may begin
its planning immediately; Story 5.4 waits on action A3.
